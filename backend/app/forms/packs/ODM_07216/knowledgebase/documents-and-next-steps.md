# ODM 07216 Documents and Next Steps

Audience: AI voice assistant helping a person know what to gather and what happens
after the application.

"who do I call". Keep answers short, then return to the current field.

## What helps to have ready (you can still apply without it)

A person can APPLY without every document — the county can follow up — but having
these handy makes the application accurate and faster:
- Social Security numbers for the people who want coverage (memorized is fine).
- Date of birth for each person.
- Income proof: recent pay stubs, or a benefit letter (Social Security, SSDI, SSI,
  unemployment, pension), or a good monthly estimate if there is no paper.
- For self-employment: a sense of monthly income and business expenses.
- Immigration document type and ID for non-citizens who want coverage and have an
  eligible status (a family member who does NOT want coverage does not need this).
- Current health insurance details, if any (plan name, member/policy ID).

Voice phrasing: "You can apply now even if you don't have every paper. Pay stubs and
Social Security numbers help, but a good estimate is fine if you don't have them yet."

## What happens after the application is submitted

1. The completed application goes to the county Department of Job and Family Services,
   which reviews it.
2. The county may ask for proof of income, identity, or immigration status. If they
   do, respond by the date in the notice so the case is not closed.
3. A decision is mailed. Ohio usually processes most applications within about 45 days
   (disability-based cases can take longer).
4. If approved, coverage can sometimes be backdated to cover medical bills from the
   last 3 months, when the person qualified then — that is why the form asks about
   recent medical bills.
5. Medicaid is renewed each year (a "redetermination"). Watch the mail and respond on
   time to keep coverage.

Voice phrasing: "After you submit, your county reviews it and may ask for a document
or two. A decision comes by mail, usually within about 45 days."

## Who to call for help

- The person's county Job and Family Services (JFS) office handles the case.
- The Ohio Medicaid Consumer Hotline can answer general questions and help locate the
  county office. Confirm the current phone number on the official Ohio Medicaid site.

Voice phrasing: "If you get stuck or have a tricky situation, your county Job and
Family Services office or the Ohio Medicaid Consumer Hotline can help."
