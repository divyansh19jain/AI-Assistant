# ODM 07216 Household and Income Guidance

Audience: AI voice assistant explaining who to include and what income to report.

Use this document when the person asks what "household", "family size",
"gross income", "self-employment", "other income", "deductions", or "expenses"
mean. Keep answers short and then return to the current field.

Keywords: household, family size, include, spouse, child, dependent, tax return,
tax filer, income, gross income, before taxes, wages, paycheck, pay period,
self-employment, business expenses, other income, Social Security, SSDI, SSI,
unemployment, pension, alimony, spousal support, child care, dependent care,
job loss, reduced hours, yearly income.

## Who to include on the application

ODM 07216 says Step 2 is where the applicant lists family members. In plain
language, include:
- the applicant;
- the spouse;
- children under age 21 who live with the applicant;
- an unmarried partner who needs health coverage;
- anyone the applicant includes on a tax return, even if that person does not
  live with the applicant;
- anyone else under age 21 whom the applicant takes care of and who lives with
  the applicant;
- anyone else who lives with the applicant but is temporarily away and has a
  definite plan to return.

The form says the applicant usually does not have to include:
- an unmarried partner who does not need health coverage, unless they have a
  common child who lives with the applicant;
- the unmarried partner's children;
- parents who live with the applicant but file their own tax returns, if the
  applicant is over age 21;
- other adult relatives who file their own tax returns.

Voice phrasing: "For this form, household does not just mean everyone under the
roof. It also looks at spouse, children, and tax relationships. I will walk you
through it."

## SSN and immigration status for people not applying

ODM 07216 says the applicant does not need to provide immigration status or a
Social Security number for family members who do not need health coverage. The
assistant should not pressure the person for those details when the schema says
they are not applicable.

## Gross income

For employment wages, the form asks for gross income before taxes per pay
period and how often the person is paid. In plain language, gross income means
the amount before taxes or deductions come out. The pay frequency can be weekly,
every two weeks, twice a month, monthly, or yearly depending on how the person
is paid.

Voice phrasing: "Gross pay means the amount before taxes are taken out. What is
the amount on the paycheck before deductions, and how often is it paid?"

## Employment and job loss

Step 3 asks whether anyone in the household is employed, self-employed, or not
employed. It also asks whether anyone left a job or had hours significantly
reduced in the past 90 days. If that happened, collect who it was and an
approximate date.

Voice phrasing: "If someone's hours were cut or they lost a job in the last
three months, that matters. Who was it, and about when did it happen?"

## Self-employment

The form asks for the person, type of work, gross income before taxes per pay
period, and frequency for self-employment. The form also notes that
self-employment expenses should be listed in the expenses question.

Voice phrasing: "Self-employment means work like running a business,
freelancing, gig work, farming, or doing jobs for yourself. What kind of work is
it, and about how much comes in?"

## Other income this month

Step 3 asks about other income types for everyone in the household. The form
lists Social Security Disability (SSDI), Social Security retirement, Social
Security survivors, unemployment compensation, pensions, spousal support,
railroad retirement, regular contributions from others, investments,
scholarships or grants, capital gains, gross farming, gross rental, royalties,
and other income.

For each type that applies, collect who receives it and the monthly amount. If
none apply, save "no" or skip according to the field.

Voice phrasing: "Other income means money that is not regular job wages. I will
ask through the common types. If nobody gets that type, just say no."

## Yearly income estimates

The form asks for yearly income only if someone's income changes from month to
month. If monthly income is steady, the person can usually skip the yearly
estimate fields when the schema allows it. If income varies, ask for the best
estimate for this calendar year and next calendar year.

Voice phrasing: "If your income changes a lot month to month, give your best
estimate for the year. It does not have to be perfect."

## Expenses

The form asks about certain expenses, including dependent care so someone can
work or go to school, child or spousal support paid, self-employment expenses,
and other listed deductions. Ask for the person, amount, and how often it is
paid when the field asks for it.

Voice phrasing: "Some expenses can matter. I will ask about child care, adult
care, support paid, and self-employment expenses."
